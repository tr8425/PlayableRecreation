[h1]Playable Recreation[/h1]

[i]Your colonists have all the fun. Take it back.[/i]

[h2]Seven things to do[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]The game of Ur[/b] — a board game from 2500 BC. Still thrilling, still infuriating
[*][b]Chess[/b] — sixty-four squares. Your opponent looks five moves ahead
[*][b]Poker[/b] — heads-up Texas hold'em. Bust your opponent to win
[*][b]Nine-ball[/b] — the balls actually roll. Hit the lowest number first
[*][b]Horseshoes[/b] — stick them on the pin. First to 21
[*][b]Hoopstone[/b] — put the stone through the ring. First to 15
[*][b]Stargazing[/b] — point the telescope at this planet's sky. The one thing here you cannot win
[/list]

All seven are base-game buildings. No DLC required. If Vanilla Furniture Expanded, GloomyFurniture or Hospitality: Casino happen to be in your list, more of their furniture becomes playable too — see below.

[h2]How it works[/h2]

Select a colonist and right-click the furniture. Selecting the furniture alone works too.

The six games each have five tiers of AI — poor to masterwork — plus a rules walkthrough, practice games against an awful opponent, and a record per tier, counted both all-time and for this colony.

Colony time keeps running. The mod never touches your game speed: space still pauses, exactly as it always did. Enter is the key that moves the board. If you would rather the colony held still, the mod can pause for you while a window is open.

When trouble arrives the window closes and the position stays on the furniture. Sit down again and you pick up where you left off, unless someone moved it, broke it, or cleaned it in the meantime. You choose which of those spoil a position.

You also choose how many undos you get. Set it to zero and there are none.

[h2]Immersion mode: two at the board[/h2]

Without it, you play and one colonist sits there. Turn [b]immersion mode[/b] on in the settings and the board gets two people at it.

[list]
[*][b]Pick who plays whom.[/b] Right-click the furniture and choose an opponent. Anyone who cannot come is greyed out with the reason — drafted, too far, already at another board
[*][b]They walk over and sit down across from each other[/b], facing each other, and stay until the game is over
[*][b]They talk while they play.[/b] Whatever they say to each other appears under the moves — the same social interactions the colony would have had anyway, in the window instead of a speech bubble
[*][b]They can leave.[/b] Hunger and tiredness get them up, and so does a threat, a draft, or someone moving the board. If your opponent dies mid-game, the game goes with them
[/list]

[b]Visitors count as opponents.[/b] Beat one or lose to one and their faction's goodwill moves a little, their mood with it. They will also sit down at an empty board on their own and wait for someone to join them — and if nobody does, they end up playing alone.

[b]Who is sitting there shows on the board.[/b] A backstory that names the game counts for that game only: a chess master plays chess well, while being a researcher does not help. Kind colonists let an undo slide. Some will flip the board when they are losing. The move list carries their names.

The difficulty you are offered comes from whoever sits across from you, not from you — so playing the colony's best is a different evening than playing its worst.

All of it is off by default, and every piece of it has its own switch.

[h2]The telescope is different[/h2]

The stars come from the seed your world was generated from, so every world has its own sky.

What you can see depends on your settlement's real latitude and longitude, the hour, and the weather. At the northern edge of the world the southern stars never rise at all. During the day the sun washes them out.

Join stars into constellations and name them. Those belong to the world, not to the telescope. Move the gravship and your latitude changes, and the sky with it.

With [b]Odyssey[/b] installed you also see what passes overhead. Standing on an asteroid or an orbital platform gives you a different sky altogether — the planet fills one side of it, and you can turn the telescope down and look at the surface instead.

[h2]Mastery[/h2]

The first time you clear a difficulty, colonists get a little more recreation and a little more skill from that piece of furniture. It accrues per game. Switch it off in the settings if you would rather it didn't.

[h2]More furniture, if you already have the mods[/h2]

[img]https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/12932015864041118073/879A36FE049709DBB7CA6536480E965100145CBC/[/img]

None of it is required. These wake up only when the mod is already in your list.

[list]
[*][b]Vanilla Furniture Expanded[/b] — the [b]darts board[/b] (five rounds, three darts each, highest total wins), the [b]punching bag[/b] (answer the beat), the [b]roulette table[/b] (a bankroll run against the house, table limit and all), and the [b]arcade machine[/b]
[*][b]GloomyFurniture[/b] — its chess table, poker table and billiards table join in
[*][b]Hospitality: Casino[/b] — sit at the [b]slot machines[/b]. The chips belong to the colony and stay in the save; silver buys more
[/list]

The arcade machine has no game of its own. Pick a difficulty and it deals you a random one — including games whose furniture you never built.

[h2]Compatibility and saves[/h2]
[list]
[*]RimWorld 1.6
[*]No DLC required. Odyssey adds more to stargazing
[*]No mod required either. Vanilla Furniture Expanded, GloomyFurniture and Hospitality: Casino are optional — supported when present, ignored when not
[*]Safe to add to and remove from existing saves. Remove it and the furniture goes back to vanilla
[*]No Harmony patches
[*]No vanilla Defs modified — one component is [i]added[/i] to the furniture, nothing else
[*]Every entry can be switched off. Switching one off only removes the right-click option; colonists still use the furniture on their own
[*]Chess and poker want a free chair at the board, the way vanilla always has
[*]English · 한국어
[*]No multiplayer support
[/list]

[h2]What about other mods, including Combat Extended?[/h2]

There are no Harmony patches and no vanilla Defs are modified — one component is [i]added[/i] to seven pieces of furniture. That leaves very little surface for conflict, but no promises beyond that.
